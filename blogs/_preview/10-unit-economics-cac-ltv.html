Unit Economics Explained: CAC, LTV and Payback Period With Real Indian Examples

Written by Roushan Gupta • 8 min read • 1,727 words
Table of Contents auto-generated by your theme

    Revenue tells you how much money came in. Unit economics tells you whether the business deserves to exist.

    A company can grow at 200% a year and lose money on every single customer it acquires. Growth then becomes a way of losing money faster — which is exactly what happened to a long list of well-funded Indian startups between 2021 and 2023. The founders who survived that period were the ones who could state their contribution margin in one sentence.

    This article breaks down the four numbers that matter, works through them with Indian examples, and shows how to fix each one when it is wrong.

    The Four Numbers That Decide Everything

    Key Takeaways

    • If contribution margin is negative, no amount of growth helps. You are paying customers to buy from you.
    • LTV to CAC of 3:1 is the widely used benchmark. Below 1:1 the business destroys value with every sale.
    • Payback period matters more than LTV for cash-strapped startups, because it decides how fast you can reinvest.
    • Discounts, free delivery and cashback are not marketing costs — they are reductions in contribution margin, and they belong in this calculation.
    • Blended numbers hide problems. Always calculate CAC by channel and LTV by segment.

    Editor's Note: The examples below use realistic but illustrative numbers to demonstrate the method. Your own figures will differ — the value here is the calculation, not the specific amounts.

    Step 1: Contribution Margin — The Number Most Founders Skip

    Contribution margin is what remains from one sale after paying for everything that varies with that sale. Fixed costs like rent and salaries are not included yet.

    Example: a D2C product sold online

    Line item Amount
    Selling price₹999
    Cost of goods−₹340
    Packaging−₹45
    Shipping (after customer contribution)−₹80
    Payment gateway fee (2%)−₹20
    Discount or coupon−₹100
    Return and RTO provision (12%)−₹120
    Contribution margin₹294 (29.4%)

    Two lines deserve attention. First, the return provision — in Indian D2C, return-to-origin rates of 10–20% are common, especially on cash-on-delivery, and founders routinely leave this out of their calculations. Second, the discount: a ₹100 coupon removed a third of your margin.

    If this number is negative, stop everything. Do not run ads. Do not raise money. Fix the price, the cost, or the return rate first.

    Step 2: Customer Acquisition Cost, Calculated Properly

    The formula people get wrong

    CAC is not "ad spend ÷ orders". It is:

    CAC = (total sales and marketing spend + salaries of the sales and marketing team + agency fees + tools) ÷ number of new customers acquired

    Note the words "new customers". Repeat purchases are not acquisitions. A business that counts every order as a new customer will believe its CAC is a third of what it actually is.

    Example: a subscription service in month one

    Step 3: Lifetime Value

    LTV is contribution margin multiplied by how many times the customer buys, or how many months they stay.

    LTV = contribution margin per order × average number of orders over the customer's life

    For a subscription business

    Average customer lifetime in months is roughly 1 ÷ monthly churn rate. If churn is 6% a month, the average customer stays about 16–17 months.

    Scenario Monthly contribution Churn Avg lifetime LTV
    Weak retention₹30012%8 months₹2,400
    Average₹3006%17 months₹5,100
    Strong retention₹3003%33 months₹9,900

    Same product, same CAC, three completely different businesses. This is why retention is worth more than acquisition for almost every early-stage company — halving churn can be worth more than doubling ad spend.

    For a one-time purchase business

    Use average orders per customer per year and the number of years they stay. A customer who buys twice a year for three years, at ₹294 contribution, has an LTV of about ₹1,764.

    Step 4: The LTV to CAC Ratio

    Ratio What it means Action
    Below 1:1You lose money on every customerStop spending immediately
    1:1 to 2:1Working, but too thin to scaleFix price, retention or channel mix
    3:1The standard healthy benchmarkScale carefully
    Above 5:1Either excellent, or under-investingConsider spending more on growth

    That last row surprises people. A ratio far above 5:1 often means you are being too conservative with marketing and leaving growth on the table.

    Step 5: Payback Period — The Cash Reality

    LTV assumes you survive long enough to collect it. Payback period asks the sharper question: how many months until the customer has paid back what you spent acquiring them?

    Payback period (months) = CAC ÷ monthly contribution margin

    Business CAC Monthly contribution Payback
    SaaS at ₹800 CAC, ₹300 margin₹800₹3002.7 months
    SaaS at ₹4,000 CAC, ₹300 margin₹4,000₹30013.3 months
    D2C at ₹1,600 CAC, ₹294 margin₹1,600₹2945.4 months

    Why it matters: with a three-month payback, every rupee you spend on acquisition comes back within a quarter, so you can reinvest it. With a thirteen-month payback, you must fund a year of losses before the customer breaks even — which requires cash you may not have.

    How to Fix Each Number When It Is Wrong

    CAC too high

    • Move budget to the cheapest channel that still converts, not the most comfortable one
    • Build a referral loop — it is usually the lowest-CAC channel available
    • Improve the landing page before increasing spend; conversion improvements cut CAC directly
    • Narrow the audience until the message is specific

    Contribution margin too low

    • Raise prices — the most underused lever in Indian startups
    • Cut the discount; test how much demand actually depends on it
    • Reduce returns: better product pages, size guides, prepaid-only offers
    • Renegotiate shipping and packaging; volume often unlocks better rates

    LTV too low

    • Attack churn before anything else — onboarding, first-week experience, support response time
    • Add an annual plan; it removes the monthly decision to leave
    • Cross-sell a second product to existing customers, whose acquisition cost is already paid
    • Build a reason to return: content, community, loyalty, consumables

    Payback too slow

    • Take payment upfront — annual plans convert payback from a year into a day
    • Reduce CAC through cheaper channels
    • Raise the monthly contribution through price

    The Mistakes That Distort All Four Numbers

    1. Counting repeat buyers as new customers. Makes CAC look far lower than it is.
    2. Leaving out salaries, agency fees and tool costs from the CAC calculation.
    3. Ignoring returns and refunds in contribution margin.
    4. Using revenue instead of contribution margin in the LTV formula — this inflates LTV by two to three times.
    5. Averaging across very different segments. A ₹500 customer and a ₹50,000 customer should never share one LTV.
    6. Forgetting the time value of money. An LTV collected over three years is not worth its face value today.

    The One-Page Sheet to Maintain

    Keep a single sheet, updated monthly, with these nine rows:

    1. New customers
    2. Total sales and marketing spend
    3. Blended CAC, and CAC by channel
    4. Average order value
    5. Contribution margin per order
    6. Monthly churn (or repeat rate)
    7. Average lifetime
    8. LTV and LTV : CAC
    9. Payback period

    Fifteen minutes a month. It is the highest-return financial habit a founder can build, because it turns every growth decision from an opinion into a calculation.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is a good LTV to CAC ratio?

    Three to one is the standard benchmark. Below two to one the business struggles to fund its own growth; above five to one you are often under-investing in acquisition.

    What if my product is a one-time purchase?

    Then your LTV is contribution margin multiplied by average purchases per customer. If customers genuinely buy once, LTV equals one order's margin, and your CAC must be comfortably below it.

    How do I calculate churn for a non-subscription business?

    Use repeat purchase rate over a fixed window, such as the percentage of customers who buy again within twelve months. It serves the same purpose.

    Should discounts count in CAC or in margin?

    In margin. A discount reduces what each order earns, which is where it belongs. Double counting it in both places will understate your economics.

    Do investors actually check these numbers?

    Serious ones check them first, before looking at growth. After the 2021–23 funding correction, unit economics became the primary filter for early-stage investment in India.

    Final Word

    Growth is a choice you make once the unit works. Until then, growth is just an accelerator on whatever you already have — including losses.

    Calculate your contribution margin this week. Then your CAC by channel. Then your payback period. If those three numbers are healthy, you have earned the right to scale. If they are not, you have found the exact thing to fix — and that discovery is worth far more than the revenue you would have grown into losing.
