Zoho vs Freshworks: Which Indian SaaS Company Is the Better Bet in 2026?

Two companies, two founders from the same state, two completely opposite playbooks. Zoho built a $1.5 billion software business without taking a single rupee of venture capital. Freshworks raised roughly $484 million, listed on Nasdaq, and is now judged every quarter by Wall Street. If you are a founder, an investor, or someone choosing business software in 2026, understanding the gap between these two is the fastest way to understand how Indian SaaS actually makes money.

This comparison looks at the founding story, the funding model, the numbers from the latest filings, the product strategy, and the culture of both companies — and ends with a plain answer on which model wins in which situation.

Key Takeaways

  • Zoho grew to ₹12,313 crore of operating revenue in FY25 while never raising venture capital — the clearest proof in India that bootstrapping can still reach global scale.
  • Freshworks is the cleaner product story: fewer, sharper tools, faster time-to-value, and a support desk product that is genuinely loved by small teams.
  • Zoho's FY25 profit actually slipped 3.3% to ₹3,191 crore because expenses grew 30.5% — a reminder that even profitable companies face margin pressure in the AI era.
  • Zoho's advantage is breadth and price. Freshworks' advantage is depth and experience.
  • For Indian founders, the real lesson is not which company is better — it is that both models work, but they demand completely different discipline.

The Founding Story: Two Very Different Starting Lines

Zoho: The Slow 30-Year Climb

Zoho did not start as Zoho. It began in 1996 as AdventNet, a network management software company started by Sridhar Vembu, a Princeton-trained engineer who had worked at Qualcomm. For its first decade the company quietly built infrastructure software for telecom operators — unglamorous work, far from headlines, but it produced something rare: real cash flow.

When Zoho shifted to business applications in the mid-2000s, it used that cash rather than outside money. CRM came first, then projects, then mail, then books, then people. Each new app was priced to undercut the American incumbents and sold directly online, without a sales army. The company also made a deliberate, unusual choice: move engineering out of Chennai into towns like Tenkasi and Kovilpatti, where salaries are lower, attrition is lower, and talent is hungry for good work.

Freshworks: The Fast, Funded Sprint

Girish Mathrubootham had spent nearly a decade at Zoho before leaving to start Freshdesk in 2010. The founding insight came from a Twitter complaint about a competitor's support product, and the first version was built in months, not years. Freshdesk raised early, raised often, and expanded from customer support into sales, marketing and IT service management.

By 2021 the company had rebranded as Freshworks and listed on Nasdaq — becoming one of the first Indian-born SaaS companies to go public in the United States. That listing brought capital, global visibility, and something else: the obligation to report growth every ninety days.

Read that table slowly. Zoho earned roughly ₹0.75 of cost for every rupee of revenue in FY25, up from ₹0.68 a year earlier. That is still an outstanding business — very few software companies anywhere run at that efficiency — but the direction of travel matters. Artificial intelligence has raised the price of computing, talent and customer acquisition for everyone.

Freshworks' story is the mirror image: a smaller revenue base, years of losses funded by investors, and only recently a shift into positive territory. The trade-off is straightforward. Freshworks bought speed and global distribution with equity. Zoho bought independence with time.

Product Strategy: The Suite Against the Specialist

Zoho's Everything-Under-One-Roof Model

Zoho sells more than fifty applications — CRM, Mail, Books, People, Desk, Analytics, Creator, Cliq, and an AI assistant called Zia. The commercial logic is simple: once a company puts its email and accounting on Zoho, moving to Zoho CRM costs almost nothing in data migration or retraining. Zoho One, the company's bundled offering, gives a business access to the entire suite for a single per-user price that undercuts buying equivalent tools separately.

The weakness of this model is obvious too. When you build fifty products, none of them can be the absolute best in its category. Zoho's CRM is excellent value; it is not Salesforce. Zoho Desk does the job; it is not always as smooth as a specialist tool.

Freshworks' Fewer-Better-Tools Model

Freshworks concentrates on four or five products: Freshdesk for support, Freshsales for sales, Freshservice for IT teams, Freshmarketer for marketing, and Freshchat for messaging. Each one is designed to be set up in an afternoon by a non-technical manager. Freddy, the company's AI layer, is woven across them.

The trade-off here is the opposite. Fewer products means less cross-sell and more exposure when a category gets commoditised. It also means Freshworks must win on product experience every single time, because it cannot bundle its way into a deal.

Go-To-Market: Self-Serve vs Enterprise Push

Zoho's motion has always been product-led. You sign up on the website, use a free tier, and pay by credit card. Enterprise sales arrived later and is still not the core. This keeps customer acquisition cost low — the company spends heavily on advertising (₹2,230 crore in FY25, up 31%) but does not need a large field sales force in every country.

Freshworks started self-serve too, but the Nasdaq listing pushed it upmarket. Larger contracts mean larger sales teams, longer cycles, and the need to prove return on investment to a finance department. The upside is bigger, stickier contracts. The downside is a slower, more expensive engine.

Culture and the Talent Question

Zoho's hiring philosophy is genuinely unusual in Indian tech. The company deliberately recruits from tier-2 and tier-3 towns, runs its own schools, and famously does not require degrees for many roles. Its Zoho Schools programme takes students after class 12, pays them a stipend, and trains them in engineering and business for years.

Freshworks competes for the same tier-1 engineering talent as every other funded company — and pays accordingly. Its culture is closer to a global product company: faster promotion cycles, stock options, higher attrition, higher output per person.

Neither is right or wrong. But they produce different companies. Zoho's model builds institutional memory and loyalty. Freshworks' model builds velocity and specialisation.

Risks Each Company Faces in 2026

  1. For Zoho: the AI transition is capital-hungry. A company that funds everything from its own cash flow moves slower when a technology shift demands spending before the revenue arrives. Its FY25 margin compression is the first visible sign.
  2. For Freshworks: public-market pressure rewards predictable growth. When growth slows, the options are cost cuts or a sale — and both are painful. Freshworks also competes head-on with giants like Salesforce and Zendesk, which have far deeper pockets.
  3. For both: generative AI is flattening the difference between a good support tool and a great one. The moat is shifting from features to data, distribution and trust.

The Bigger Lesson for Indian Founders

Strip away the products and you are left with a single question every founder must answer: are you optimising for control or for speed?

Zoho optimised for control. It gave up the chance to grow three times faster in the 2010s in exchange for never having to explain itself to a board of investors. Three decades later it is worth more than most listed peers and answers to nobody.

Freshworks optimised for speed. It reached global scale and public-market recognition in eleven years — a pace Zoho could never have matched. The price is permanent accountability to shareholders.

Both won. The mistake founders make is copying the tactics of one while holding the expectations of the other. If you are bootstrapping, you cannot spend like a funded company. If you are funded, you cannot grow at bootstrapped speed and expect patience.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Zoho an Indian company or an American company?

Zoho Corporation is headquartered in the United States, but its ownership, founders, and the overwhelming majority of its engineering workforce are Indian. Its main development centres are in Chennai, Tenkasi and Kovilpatti in Tamil Nadu.

Has Zoho ever raised venture capital?

No. Zoho has never taken institutional venture capital and has repeatedly stated it has no plans to list. It has grown entirely on its own revenue.

Which is cheaper, Zoho or Freshworks?

For a single tool, prices are broadly comparable. For a full business stack, Zoho is far cheaper, because Zoho One bundles dozens of applications into one subscription while Freshworks products are priced individually.

Is Freshworks profitable now?

Freshworks has moved into profitability after years of losses, but its margin structure is thinner and more volatile than Zoho's, largely because it spends far more on sales and marketing as a percentage of revenue.

Should I worry about either company shutting down?

Neither is at risk. Zoho has cash reserves and no debt to service. Freshworks is publicly listed with transparent financials. The realistic risk for a buyer is product direction, not survival.

Final Verdict

If you want the best software experience for one specific job — support, or sales — Freshworks will usually feel better in the first ninety days. If you want the best value across an entire business over five years, Zoho wins by a wide margin.

But the more useful comparison is not product versus product. It is philosophy versus philosophy. Zoho proves that patience compounds. Freshworks proves that capital accelerates. India produced both, in the same decade, from the same talent pool — and that is the part of the story worth studying.
